## Document purpose

When an entrepreneur clicks **Marketing**, they are not opening software. They are entering **Emma's office**.

This document defines what that office contains, what it never contains, and why. It is the template for every future peer workspace — Sales, Support, Planner, Finance, Analytics, CEO — with domain-specific content plugged into the same architectural skeleton.

**Home Command Center is design frozen (PX-21).** The Marketing Workspace inherits its executive quality bar but operates at **peer scope**: one colleague, one business domain, one trust relationship.

---

## 1. Workspace Philosophy

### The office, not the tool

Peergent is the **AI Workforce Operating System**. Users do not manage campaigns, pipelines, or tickets. They collaborate with **Emma** — a digital marketing colleague who owns outcomes in her domain.

The Marketing Workspace is therefore:

- **A colleague's command center**, not a feature dashboard
- **Business-first**, not process-first
- **Calm by default**, deep on demand
- **Honest** — real data or silence, never decoration

When the entrepreneur arrives, they should feel what they feel opening a trusted advisor's briefing: *"She's on it. I know where we stand. I know if she needs me."*

### Executive Workspace, not KPI wall

We embrace dashboards — but **executive dashboards**. That means:

| We build | We reject |
|----------|-----------|
| One glance orientation | Metric grids without narrative |
| Premium KPI cards with context | Sparkline walls |
| One chart, one story | Chart galleries |
| Emma's voice in briefings | System status lines |
| Recommendations with business rationale | Generic AI suggestions |
| White space and hierarchy | Equal visual weight everywhere |
| Decisions surfaced when necessary | Approval queues as homepage |

The Workspace is allowed to show numbers. Numbers must **answer business questions**, not prove the product is "data-driven."

### Relationship to Home

| Home (org scope) | Marketing Workspace (peer scope) |
|------------------|----------------------------------|
| Whole workforce | Emma only |
| Cross-peer attention | Emma-scoped decisions |
| Company briefing | Emma's briefing |
| Business impact (org) | Business impact (marketing contribution) |
| Live activity (all peers) | Emma's recent movement |
| Navigation to peers | Navigation within Emma's domain |

**Duplication rule:** If something appears on Home *and* in Emma's Workspace, the Workspace version is **deeper and peer-specific**. Home never links into Workflow. Home never shows campaign internals.

### Design extraction (principles, not layouts)

From **Linear:** one primary action per view state; status is ambient, never the hero.  
From **Stripe:** numbers are precise or absent; empty states are honest.  
From **Vercel:** premium restraint — typography and spacing do the work.  
From **Arc:** spatial consistency — one shell, predictable tab memory.  
From **Inspace:** outcomes lead; AI presence is calm ("Autonomous · live"), not theatrical.  
From **Apple Intelligence:** intelligence feels **personal and contextual**, not like a chatbot bolted onto settings.

We do not copy any of these layouts. We copy their **confidence**.

### The arrival moment

The entrepreneur's mental model when landing:

> *"I'm checking in with Emma."*

Not:

> *"I'm opening the marketing module."*

Everything in this bible serves that single emotional contract.

## 2. Emma's Responsibilities

Emma is the **Marketing Brain** embodied as a colleague. Her responsibilities are **outcome-owned**, not tool-owned.

### Core mandate

**Grow the business through marketing** — with judgment, within agreed boundaries, with the entrepreneur's trust.

### What Emma owns

| Domain | Emma's accountability (customer language) |
|--------|----------------------------------------|
| **Market understanding** | Knows the audience, competitors, and positioning well enough to recommend |
| **Strategy** | Proposes where to play, on which channels, with what message |
| **Campaign planning** | Turns strategy into executable plans with clear business intent |
| **Creative direction** | Ensures content matches brand and strategy before production |
| **Content production** | Drafts, adapts, and prepares publish-ready marketing assets |
| **Publishing** | Executes approved content to connected channels within autonomy rules |
| **Performance interpretation** | Reads results and explains what they mean for the business |
| **Optimization** | Recommends budget, channel, and message adjustments based on evidence |
| **Brand consistency** | Applies brand voice and visual rules across outputs |
| **Escalation** | Surfaces decisions that require human judgment — nothing else |

### What Emma does not own

- Company-wide financial decisions (Finance peer / entrepreneur)
- Sales conversation and deal closure (Sales peer)
- Customer support resolution (Support peer)
- Org-wide planning cadence (Planner peer)
- Technical infrastructure, runtime, or Brain registry (admin only)

### Autonomy contract (experience expression)

Emma operates in modes the **entrepreneur configures once** in Settings:

| Mode | Customer feeling | Emma's behavior |
|------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Recommend** | "She advises; I decide." | Prepares work; waits before irreversible steps |
| **Collaborate** | "She drafts; I approve key moments." | Acts within plan; stops at configured gates |
| **Autonomous** | "She runs; I review results." | Executes within scope; reports outcomes |

The Workspace must **never** expose mode names as technical toggles on every screen. Presence communicates mode: *"Autonomous · live"* or *"Waiting for your approval on strategy."*

### Emma's character

- **Professional warmth** — competent, calm, never casual to the point of flippancy
- **First person** — "I prepared…", "I recommend…", "I need your decision on…"
- **Specific** — always names the campaign, channel, or business outcome
- **Honest about gaps** — "I can't measure ROAS yet because Meta isn't connected."
- **Never performative** — no fake enthusiasm, no exclamation spam, no "Amazing news!!!"

## 3. What Entrepreneurs Actually Care About

Entrepreneurs are not marketers by trade. They hire Emma because marketing is **important and overwhelming**. Their questions are business questions.

### Primary questions (in priority order)

1. **Is marketing working?** — Revenue influenced, leads, ROAS, growth, brand reach
2. **Is Emma doing her job?** — Visible progress, credible presence, no surprises
3. **Do I need to do anything?** — One clear answer: yes (with one action) or no (with calm silence)
4. **Are we spending wisely?** — Budget efficiency, channel mix, waste avoidance
5. **What's live right now?** — Published campaigns, active spend, public-facing content
6. **What should we do next?** — One recommendation, not a menu of options
7. **Can I trust the outputs?** — Brand-safe, strategically aligned, professionally written

### Secondary questions (power users, less frequent)

8. What campaigns exist and how are they structured?  
9. Show me the content library.  
10. How did Emma decide this? (Workflow — on demand)  
11. Connect or fix integrations.  
12. Adjust how much Emma can do alone.

### What entrepreneurs do not care about (in default path)

- Pipeline stage names ("reasoning", "validation node")  
- Work units, artifacts, deliverable IDs  
- Review queue mechanics  
- Evidence graphs and continuation diagnostics  
- Percent-complete bars without business meaning  
- Tables of every draft ever created  
- "Processing" without saying *what*

### The sixty-second test

An entrepreneur must be able to open Marketing → Workspace and within **sixty seconds** answer:

- What is Emma working on?
- Did marketing create value recently?
- Do I need to approve anything?
- What does Emma recommend?

If any answer requires opening Workflow or reading a timeline, the Workspace has failed.

## 4. Daily Workflow of Emma

This is **Emma's day**, described as the entrepreneur experiences it — not as the Brain executes it.

### Morning (before the entrepreneur arrives)

Emma may:

- Check performance signals from connected channels  
- Identify anomalies or opportunities (spend spike, post outperforming)  
- Continue in-flight campaign work within autonomy  
- Prepare deliverables queued for the entrepreneur's review  
- Update her internal understanding from market/competitor research (invisible)

The entrepreneur **does not** receive notifications for every internal step.

### When the entrepreneur arrives

**Default path:** Workspace.

Emma shows:

1. Presence — what she's focused on right now  
2. Briefing — what moved since last visit (if anything meaningful)  
3. Business impact — marketing contribution in numbers (if data exists)  
4. Decisions — only if something truly needs judgment (max 3)  
5. Recommendation — one insight with business rationale  

### During the day

| Entrepreneur action | Emma's response (experience) |
|---------------------|------------------------------|
| Approves strategy | "I'll start creative direction." — visible on Work, not a toast spam |
| Ignores recommendation | Emma stays quiet; recommendation ages gracefully, does not nag |
| Opens a campaign | Briefing-first campaign view; business context before assets |
| Connects LinkedIn | Blocked items unlock; Emma narrates what she can now do |
| Checks Performance | Outcomes and trends; optimization suggestions if confident |

### End of day

Done outcomes appear in Workspace briefing footer and Performance — **not** as a system log. Format: *"Published 3 LinkedIn posts · influenced 12 visits"* — not *"Execution completed for deliverable X."*

### Weekly rhythm

Emma's Workspace should support a **weekly check-in** pattern:

- **Monday:** Orientation — what's live, what's planned, what needs you  
- **Mid-week:** Light — only interrupt if decision required  
- **Friday:** Value summary — contribution, wins, one recommendation for next week  

Performance tab carries the weekly narrative; Workspace carries the daily pulse.

## 5. Workspace Information Hierarchy

Information has a **strict priority order**. Higher bands win attention; lower bands defer or hide when empty.

### Global hierarchy (all Marketing pages)

```
1. Presence        — Who is Emma right now? (working, waiting, autonomous, blocked)
2. Attention       — Does the entrepreneur need to act? (only if yes)
3. Outcomes        — What value was created? (numbers + narrative)
4. Direction       — What is Emma doing and why? (briefing, active work summary)
5. Recommendation  — What does Emma suggest next? (one)
6. Records         — Campaigns, content, history (on demand via tabs)
7. Process         — Workflow, timelines, evidence (Workflow tab only)
```

**Rule:** Never invert layers 6 and 7 above layers 1–5 on any primary destination.

### Per-page hierarchy

| Page | Leads with | Never leads with |
|------|------------|------------------|
| **Workspace** | Presence + briefing | Campaign table |
| **Work** | Active engagements (outcome-framed) | Stage filters |
| **Content** | Visual previews of live and draft assets | File list |
| **Performance** | Hero KPI + one chart + insight | Provider configuration |
| **Workflow** | Current pipeline position (plain language) | Raw node graph |
| **Settings** | Working agreement summary | Advanced toggles |

### Attention budget

| Surface | Max decision items visible | Overflow |
|---------|---------------------------|----------|
| Workspace | 3 | Link to Inbox (org) — "2 more across your team" |
| Campaign detail | 1 primary decision | Secondary in collapsed section |
| Work row | 0 (link to decision surface) | — |
| Content card | 1 inline approve if draft | — |

### Silence is a feature

When Emma needs nothing:

- No empty decision cards  
- No "All caught up!" billboards  
- Presence + briefing + impact still communicate life  

Calm silence reads as competence. Noise reads as anxiety.

## 6. Navigation Architecture

### The six destinations

```
Workspace
    ↓
Work
    ↓
Content
    ↓
Performance
    ↓
Workflow
    ↓
Settings
```

**Tab order rationale:** Default to command center; descend from **outcomes in motion** (Work) → **artifacts** (Content) → **results** (Performance) → **process transparency** (Workflow) → **configuration** (Settings).

Badge **only** on Workspace when decisions exist (or global Inbox badge in org shell — never both screaming).

---

### Workspace

**Question answered:** *"What's happening with marketing right now, and do I need to be involved?"*

**Why it exists:** This is Emma's **front door** — the peer-scoped equivalent of Home Command Center. Delegation-first entrepreneurs may live entirely here.

**Why it is default landing:** Entrepreneurs click Marketing to **check in**, not to browse campaigns. The first screen must reward that intent in one glance.

**What belongs here:**

- Emma presence (name, role, status, mode)  
- Executive briefing (what moved, what's next in plain language)  
- Business impact band (marketing contribution KPIs + one chart)  
- Decisions band (max 3, only if non-empty)  
- Recommendation (one card)  
- Active work summary (3–5 rows — **not** the full Work list)  
- Recent outcomes (2–3 done lines with impact labels)  

**What does not belong here:**

- Full campaign database  
- Workflow timeline  
- Content grid  
- Settings shortcuts beyond one "Connect channels" nudge when blocked  
- Duplicate of Home org-wide metrics  

**Relationship to other tabs:** Workspace **summarizes**; Work, Content, Performance **expand**. Workflow is never linked from Workspace hero bands.

---

### Work

**Question answered:** *"What is Emma responsible for, and what's the state of each engagement?"*

**Why it exists:** Power users and growing businesses need to see **all active marketing engagements** without admin complexity. Work is the **portfolio view** of Emma's responsibilities.

**Why it is not the default:** Listing campaigns first trains users to **manage software**. Checking in with Emma trains them to **delegate**.

**What belongs here:**

- Outcome-framed **engagement rows** (Campaign is the Marketing noun)  
- Grouping: **Needs you · In progress · Live · Quiet · Completed**  
- One-line business status per row (*"Live on LinkedIn · €240 spend this week"*)  
- Search and filter (power disclosure)  
- Entry to campaign detail (briefing-first drill-down)  

**What does not belong here:**

- Workflow step indicators as primary row content  
- Percent-complete bars without business meaning  
- Work unit lists  
- Draft file counts as hero metrics  
- Technical blocked reasons (*"Node 7 failed"*) — translate to *"Waiting for LinkedIn connection"*  

**Critical distinction:** Work is **not** "what Emma is typing right now." That lives in Workspace presence and briefing. Work is **what Emma owns** — the engagements themselves.

---

### Content

**Question answered:** *"What has Emma created, and what's live in market?"*

**Why it exists:** Marketing is visual. Entrepreneurs want to **see** posts, emails, ads — not read filenames. Content is Emma's **creative studio wall**.

**Why it is separate from Work:** Campaigns are containers; content is **what the market sees**. Entrepreneurs often ask "what did we publish this week?" without naming a campaign.

**What belongs here:**

- Preview-first cards (aspect-ratio thumbnails, channel badge)  
- Filters: Channel · Status (Draft · Scheduled · Live) · Campaign  
- Primary actions: Preview · Approve · View live (when published)  
- Empty state: Emma voice — *"No content yet — I'll start once you approve the strategy for [Campaign]."*  

**What does not belong here:**

- Spreadsheet-style tables  
- Sortable columns of metadata  
- Bulk edit chrome as default  
- Evidence attachments as primary UI  

**Publishing-first hierarchy:** Live content ranks above drafts in default sort. Drafts awaiting approval surface in a **"Waiting for you"** strip at top (max 3), not a separate nav tab.

---

### Performance

**Question answered:** *"Is marketing contributing to the business, and where should we adjust?"*

**Why it exists:** Entrepreneurs fund marketing to **grow the business**. This tab closes the loop between Emma's work and commercial outcomes.

**Why it is not on Workspace alone:** Workspace shows **snapshot impact** (hero KPIs + one chart). Performance is the **deep read** — trends, channel breakdown, optimization history.

**What belongs here:**

- Hero KPI band (3–5 metrics max): influenced revenue, leads, ROAS, spend efficiency, content reach — **only connected, honest metrics**  
- One primary trend chart with period toggle (7d / 30d / 90d)  
- Insight sentence beneath chart (*"LinkedIn drove 62% of leads this month"*)  
- Recommendation band (optimization — one primary suggestion)  
- Channel breakdown (secondary, progressive disclosure)  
- Empty/honest states per integration  

**What does not belong here:**

- Integration OAuth flows (Settings → Connections)  
- Workflow or campaign stage data  
- Raw API debug panels  
- Comparison to fake benchmarks  

**Visit frequency:** Lower than Workspace. Tab is **available, not promoted** on every arrival.

---

### Workflow

**Question answered:** *"How did Emma think through this, and where is she in the process?"*

**Why it exists:** The Brain pipeline is deep. Some entrepreneurs — and all operators during debugging — need **transparency without making process the hero**.

**Why it is its own page (not on Workspace):**

- Process vocabulary **erodes trust** when shown too early  
- The pipeline is **long** (Research → … → Performance) — it belongs in a dedicated transparency zone  
- Power users choose depth; delegation users never need to visit  
- Separating Workflow **protects** the executive feel of Workspace  

**What belongs here:**

- Plain-language pipeline stages (see Chapter 13)  
- Current position for active engagement(s)  
- Collapsed history — expand for "how Emma decided"  
- Links to evidence **one level deep**, not graphs as wallpaper  
- Admin/dev diagnostics behind role flag only  

**What does not belong here:**

- Primary approve buttons (those live in Workspace, Campaign, Content, Inbox)  
- KPI dashboards (Performance)  
- Content previews (Content)  

**Default sort:** Active engagements first. Completed pipelines archived, searchable.

---

### Settings

**Question answered:** *"How does Emma work, and what does she know?"*

**Why it exists:** Configuration is **configure once, rarely revisit**. It must not pollute daily command center bands.

**What belongs here:**

- Working agreement (autonomy, approval policy)  
- Knowledge & brand (voice, guidelines, ICP alignment)  
- Connections (LinkedIn, Meta, GA4, email, CRM)  
- Notifications (what Emma may interrupt you for)  
- Advanced (responsibilities, locale, power options)  

**What does not belong here:**

- Performance charts  
- Campaign lists  
- Workflow timeline as default section  

**Hierarchy:** Most entrepreneurs touch **Connections** and **Working agreement** once. Everything else is progressive disclosure.

## 7. Workspace Bands

Think in **information bands**, not components. Bands appear top-to-bottom in this order. **Omit empty bands entirely** — never render placeholder shells.

### Band A — Presence

**Information:** Emma · Marketing · status pill · optional mode chip (`Autonomous · live`)

**Copy pattern:** One first-person sentence — *"I'm optimizing your LinkedIn campaign based on last week's results."*

**Optional second line:** *"Then: draft email sequence for Product launch."* — only if confident and useful.

**Never:** Percent complete, phase chips, animated loaders without active work.

---

### Band B — Executive Briefing

**Information:** What changed since last meaningful visit — accomplishments, context, momentum.

**Structure:**

```
Briefing van Emma
─────────────────
[Salutation — optional, only first visit of day]

[Intro — 1–2 sentences, business language]

• Accomplishment line (checkmark)
• Accomplishment line
• Accomplishment line

[Footer stats — optional: revenue influenced · tasks completed · pending count]
```

**Max visible:** 3 accomplishment lines before "View full briefing" expansion.

**Voice:** Emma, first person, past tense for done, present for ongoing.

**Never:** Bullet list of workflow steps; "Runtime completed 3 nodes."

---

### Band C — Business Impact

**Information:** Marketing's contribution to the business — the peer-scoped mirror of Home's Business Impact band.

**Structure:**

```
Beïnvloede omzet (or domain-appropriate hero label)
─────────────────
[Hero metric + delta pill]

[Trend chart — single series, 30d default]

[One insight sentence — "so what"]
```

**Rules:**

- One chart only  
- Metrics must be sourced — demo mode explicit in demo environments only  
- If integrations missing: honest empty — *"Koppel Google Analytics om marketingbijdrage te zien."* + one Settings link  

**Never:** KPI wall of six equal cards with no hierarchy.

---

### Band D — Decisions (conditional)

**Information:** Items requiring entrepreneur judgment — **only renders when count > 0**.

**Structure:** Same executive approval module language as Home "Wacht op jou" — max 3 cards, peer-scoped.

**Card information per item:**

- Title (business action: *"LinkedIn-budgetvoorstel bevestigen"*)  
- Consequence line (*"Dan kan ik spend verhogen en leads meten."*)  
- Age  
- CTA: *"Bekijk →"*  

**Overflow:** *"Nog 2 in Inbox"* — link to org Inbox filtered to Emma.

**Never:** Render empty decision band. Never show 8 items.

---

### Band E — Recommendation

**Information:** One insight Emma believes is worth acting on.

**Structure:**

```
Emma beveelt aan
────────────────
[Headline — business recommendation]

[Impact line — optional, one metric or outcome]

[Primary CTA — single]
```

**Rules:**

- Max **one** recommendation on Workspace  
- If a decision exists in Band D, recommendation may defer (no competing CTAs)  
- If confidence low, omit band entirely  

**Never:** Stack of suggestion cards. Never generic "Try using AI more."

---

### Band F — Active Work Summary

**Information:** 3–5 engagement rows — **preview of Work tab**, not replacement.

**Row format:**

```
[Campaign name]
[One outcome line — "Live · 3 posts this week" or "Waiting for strategy approval"]
[Optional channel dot]
```

**Footer:** *"All work →"* links to Work tab.

**Never:** Full campaign grid. Never stage progress bars.

---

### Band G — Recent Outcomes

**Information:** 2–3 completed items with **impact label**.

**Format:** *"Campaign strategy approved"* · *"Influenced €1,2k pipeline"* · link

**Grouping:** Today only on Workspace; fuller history in Performance and Work completed group.

**Never:** Raw activity log. Never duplicate Home Live Activity verbatim — peer-scoped only.

---

### Band spacing rhythm

Vertical gap between major bands: **40px**  
Gutter between cards in a band: **24px**  
Internal card padding: **24px**  
Band internal element gap: **16px**  
Tight metadata gap: **8px**  

Same rhythm as Home Command Center (PX-21 frozen).

## 8. Executive Briefing Philosophy

### Purpose

The briefing is Emma's **voice in the room** — the signature moment that separates Peergent from every automation tool. It answers: *"What happened that I should know?"*

### Tone

| Attribute | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| Register | Professional warmth |
| Person | First person (Emma) |
| Length | Intro ≤ 2 sentences; bullets ≤ 3 visible |
| Confidence | State facts; flag uncertainty explicitly |
| Celebration | Restrained — outcomes speak, not hype |

**Good:** *"I published three LinkedIn posts this week. Early signals show strong engagement from founders in Benelux."*

**Bad:** *"Great news! Your posts are live!!! 🚀"*

### Structure rules

1. **Lead with business movement**, not process  
2. **Accomplishments are outcomes**, not steps (*"Strategy approved"* not *"Completed planning node"*)  
3. **Footer stats optional** — only when they add confidence (revenue influenced, pending count)  
4. **Salutation once per day** — *"Goedemorgen"* acceptable; not every refresh  

### When Emma interrupts

Emma **speaks in briefing** when:

- Something meaningful completed since last visit  
- A campaign went live  
- Performance crossed a threshold Emma is configured to mention  
- A blocked state was resolved  

Emma **does not speak** when:

- Internal research ran with no customer-visible output  
- She reformatted a draft minorly  
- The entrepreneur refreshed twice in ten minutes with no change  

### When Emma stays quiet

If nothing moved:

- Briefing collapses to one calm line: *"Alles loopt volgens plan. Ik werk verder aan [Campaign]."*  
- Or briefing band omitted entirely — Presence band carries the narrative  

**Anti-pattern:** Repeating the same briefing on every page load. Briefing must be **session-aware** or **change-aware**.

### Briefing vs. Recommendation

| Briefing | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Reports what happened | Proposes what to do next |
| Backward-looking + current state | Forward-looking |
| May have no CTA | Always has one CTA |
| Multiple accomplishment lines OK | Exactly one headline |

Never merge into one wall of text.

## 9. Business Impact Philosophy

### Purpose

Business Impact answers: **"Is Emma worth it?"** — in numbers the entrepreneur already uses to run the business.

### KPIs that matter (Marketing)

| KPI | When to show | When to hide |
|-----|--------------|--------------|
| **Influenced revenue** | CRM or attribution connected | Hide or show "Connect CRM" |
| **Leads generated** | Forms, ads, or CRM connected | Hide |
| **ROAS / spend efficiency** | Paid channels connected | Hide |
| **Cost per lead** | Paid + lead tracking | Hide |
| **Content reach / impressions** | Social/analytics connected | Hide |
| **Time saved** | Always available if Brain tracks hours | Show with methodology label |

### KPIs that do not belong

- Tokens used  
- Model invocations  
- Work units completed (internal)  
- Approval turnaround time (unless entrepreneur explicitly wants ops metrics)  
- "AI confidence score"  

### Chart philosophy (peer scope)

Same rules as Home (Vision v13):

- **One chart, one story** on Workspace  
- Up to **three charts** on Performance — each with distinct question  
- Line charts for trends; bar for channel comparison  
- **Forbidden:** pie charts, gauge walls, sparkline grids without labels  
- **Insight line mandatory** beneath chart — the "so what"  

### Chart questions (examples)

| Chart | Question it answers |
|-------|---------------------|
| Influenced revenue over 30d | Are we trending up? |
| Leads by channel | Where should we invest? |
| Spend vs. ROAS | Are we efficient? |

### Honesty contract

Stripe principle: **real or absent**.

- No interpolated fake trends in production  
- Demo environment may use labeled demo data  
- Partial data shows range with caveat — *"Based on LinkedIn only — connect Meta for full picture."*

## 10. Work Philosophy

### The cardinal rule

**Work is NOT a campaign database.**

Work is: **"What Emma is currently working on."**

The entrepreneur should scan Work and feel **momentum and ownership**, not inventory management.

### Mental model

Think **editorial portfolio**, not **project management grid**.

Each row answers:

- What is this? (Campaign name — business language)  
- What's the state in human terms? (Live · Waiting for you · Preparing)  
- Why does it matter? (One outcome line)  

### Grouping (default)

```
Needs you          ← decisions embedded in engagement context
In progress        ← Emma actively working
Live               ← published / spending / in market
Quiet              ← scheduled or paused, no action needed
Completed          ← collapsed by default, last 30 days visible
```

Groups are **business states**, not Brain stages.

### Row information architecture

**Visible by default:**

- Campaign title  
- One status sentence (Emma voice or neutral business language)  
- Channel indicators (small dots — max 3)  
- Updated timestamp (relative)  

**Hidden until row expand or drill-down:**

- Budget details  
- Deliverable list  
- Workflow position  
- Version history  

### Entry to campaign

Clicking a row opens **Campaign Experience** (Chapter 15) — always **briefing-first**, never timeline-first.

### Empty states

| State | Emma says |
|-------|-----------|
| No campaigns | *"I don't have an active campaign yet. Want me to propose one based on your goals?"* + one CTA |
| All completed | *"All campaigns are wrapped. Here's what I'd suggest next."* + recommendation link |
| Blocked (no connections) | *"I can't publish until LinkedIn is connected."* + Settings link |

### Search and filter (power)

Available but **not default focus**. Filter by channel, status, date — never by "workflow stage."

## 11. Content Philosophy

### Studio, not storage

Content is where marketing **becomes real** — visuals, copy, previews. The entrepreneur should feel like walking past a **creative studio wall**, not opening Google Drive.

### Preview-first

Every content item renders as:

- Visual preview (thumbnail, copy excerpt, email subject preview)  
- Channel badge (LinkedIn, Meta, Email, Web)  
- Status pill (Draft · Scheduled · Live · Needs approval)  
- Campaign association (secondary line)  

**No file names as titles.** Title is the **business headline** of the piece.

### Publishing-first sort

Default order:

1. Live in market (most recent first)  
2. Scheduled  
3. Drafts needing approval  
4. Other drafts  

### Approval in context

Drafts requiring approval show **inline approve** on hover/expand — not a redirect to a review queue page as first action.

Full review opens **Review surface** (shared Peergent pattern — review bar never disappears during review).

### Filters

Simple chip filters: **All · LinkedIn · Email · Ads · Live · Drafts**

No advanced query builder in default view.

### Empty state

*"No content yet. Once you approve the strategy for [Campaign], I'll start creating posts and emails here."*

### Relationship to Work

- Work row may show *"3 drafts ready"* — links to Content filtered to that campaign  
- Content never shows campaign **workflow** — only campaign **name** as tag

## 12. Performance Philosophy

### Business outcomes, not analytics SaaS

Performance is **Emma's report to the business** — not a BI tool with Emma's avatar pasted on.

### Page structure (bands)

```
Band 1 — Hero KPIs (3–5 max, hierarchical — one hero metric dominant)
Band 2 — Primary trend chart + insight
Band 3 — Recommendation (optimization)
Band 4 — Channel breakdown (collapsible)
Band 5 — Historical wins (narrative, optional)
```

### Metric hierarchy

**Hero metric** (largest): the one KPI the entrepreneur optimizes for — configurable in Settings (default: influenced revenue or leads, depending on business model).

**Supporting metrics** (smaller): contextual — ROAS, CPL, reach.

### Marketing contribution framing

Always answer:

- What did marketing **influence**?  
- At what **cost**?  
- Compared to **when**?  
- What should we **do next**?  

### Optimization recommendations

Sourced from Brain performance interpretation layer — presented as:

*"I recommend shifting 20% of budget from Meta to LinkedIn — ROAS is 2.1× higher there over the last 14 days."*

One primary recommendation visible. Others behind "More insights."

### Integration honesty

Each metric shows **source badge** (GA4, LinkedIn, HubSpot) — small, tertiary.

Disconnected sources show **connect prompt**, not zero with no explanation.

### Visit cadence design

Performance is optimized for **weekly review**, not hourly monitoring. No real-time tickers. No flashing deltas.

## 13. Workflow Philosophy

### Why Workflow exists

The Brain pipeline is Emma's **thinking apparatus**. Entrepreneurs rarely need to see it — but when trust is forming, when something goes wrong, or when a power user wants depth, **transparency must exist**.

Workflow is the **confession booth of competence**: Emma shows her work without making process the product.

### The pipeline (customer language)

Map internal Brain layers to **plain-language stages**:

```
Research          → "Understanding your market"
Reasoning         → "Analyzing options"
Marketing Intel   → "Reading competitors and trends"
Brand             → "Applying your brand"
Strategy          → "Choosing direction"
Planning          → "Building the plan"
Creative          → "Creating content"
Publishing        → "Going live"
Performance       → "Measuring results"
```

**Never show** internal capability names, node IDs, or executor labels in default view.

### Why this is not on Workspace

| If pipeline were on Workspace… | Consequence |
|-------------------------------|-------------|
| Process visible first | Product feels like automation software |
| Long vertical timeline | Destroys calm executive composition |
| Technical vocabulary leaks | Trust drops for non-technical entrepreneurs |
| Constantly updating steps | Anxiety — "why is she still on step 4?" |

Workspace shows **outcomes of pipeline** (*"Strategy approved"*). Workflow shows **pipeline itself** for those who choose to look.

### Workflow page structure

```
Active engagements
──────────────────
[Campaign name]
[Current stage — plain language]
[Progress narrative — 1 sentence]
[Expand: stage history]

Completed engagements
───────────────────
(collapsed, searchable)
```

### Expand behavior

Expanded stage history:

- Stage name · completed date · one-line output summary  
- Link: *"See how Emma decided"* → evidence summary (not raw JSON)  
- Admin: full inspector link (role-gated)  

### When Workflow updates

Stages advance **silently** in background. Workflow page reflects state on visit — no push notifications per stage.

Only **stage failures** and **approval gates** interrupt the entrepreneur (via Workspace Decisions band).

## 14. Settings Philosophy

### Configure once

Settings is for **working agreement** — how Emma behaves, what she knows, what she's connected to. Not daily operations.

### Category hierarchy

```
1. Working agreement     — autonomy, approval gates, spend limits
2. Knowledge & brand     — voice, guidelines, audience
3. Connections           — OAuth integrations
4. Notifications         — interrupt rules
5. Advanced              — responsibilities, tools, locale
```

### Default landing

**Working agreement summary** — current mode in plain language:

*"Emma prepares campaigns and waits for your approval before publishing."*

One primary edit path per section — no settings dashboard of 40 tiles.

### Connections prominence

Blocked capabilities **anywhere in product** must link here — never to a modal maze.

Connection cards:

- Service name · status · last sync  
- One action: Connect / Reconnect / Disconnect  

### What never appears in customer Settings

- Brain registry  
- Capability dependency graph  
- Runtime toggles  
- Prompt editors  
- Token limits  
- Model selection  

Admin/dev surfaces only.

## 15. Campaign Experience Philosophy

### Briefing-first, business-first

Opening a campaign is **not** opening a project management view. It is reading **Emma's briefing on this engagement**.

### Campaign page bands (in order)

```
1. Campaign presence     — name, status sentence, channel, live since
2. Campaign briefing     — why this campaign exists, strategy summary
3. Decision (if needed)  — one primary approval
4. Business snapshot     — spend, leads, ROAS for this campaign (if live)
5. Content preview strip — 3–6 recent assets, link to Content filtered
6. Recommendation        — Emma's next move for this campaign
7. Disclosure            — "Timeline & details" collapsed
```

**Collapsed disclosure contains:** version history, workflow position link, budget details, team notes — **never visible by default**.

### NOT workflow-first

**Forbidden as default campaign hero:**

- 10-step lifecycle bar  
- Phase grid with percentages  
- Deliverable checklist as primary content  
- Work unit list  

These exist only inside disclosure for power users.

### Campaign states (customer language)

| Internal complexity | Customer label |
|--------------------|----------------|
| Strategy in review | Waiting for your approval on strategy |
| Creative in progress | Creating content |
| Scheduled | Scheduled to go live [date] |
| Live | Live since [date] |
| Optimizing | Improving based on results |
| Paused | Paused — [reason in business language] |
| Completed | Completed · [outcome summary] |

### Navigation back

Always one clear path: **← Work** or breadcrumb *Work / Campaign name* — never orphan pages.

## 16. Approval Philosophy

### Interrupt only when necessary

Emma asks for judgment **only at gates that matter**:

| Gate | Why interrupt |
|------|---------------|
| Strategy approval | Direction commitment — hard to reverse |
| Budget / spend | Financial boundary |
| Publish to public | Reputation risk |
| Brand-sensitive creative | Voice and compliance |
| Material ICP/positioning change | Business truth change |

Emma does **not** ask for:

- Internal research completion  
- Draft formatting  
- Analytics pulls  
- Competitor read summaries (unless configured)  

### Approval surfaces (priority)

1. **Workspace Decisions band** — peer-scoped, max 3  
2. **Org Inbox** — cross-peer queue  
3. **Campaign decision band** — contextual to engagement  
4. **Content inline approve** — lightweight draft approval  
5. **Review surface** — full deliverable review with review bar  

**Single source of truth per item** — same approval must not show different copy in four places.

### Approval card information

- **Title:** business action  
- **Consequence:** what Emma does after approval  
- **Age:** relative time  
- **Context link:** optional — see campaign  
- **CTA:** *"Bekijk →"* / *"Review"* — one verb  

### Decline and feedback

Sending back must feel like **collaboration**:

*"Emma will revise based on your feedback."* — not *"Request rejected."*

### After approval

Emma narrates next step **once** in briefing — not push notification + email + badge + modal.

## 17. Design Rules

### NEVER appear (customer Marketing paths)

- Work unit, artifact, deliverable ID, runtime, executor, planner  
- Review queue, processing, pending (without *what*)  
- Percent-complete bars without business label  
- Phase chip grids as hero content  
- Pie charts, gauge walls, fake metrics  
- Tables as default content layout  
- Multiple competing primary CTAs  
- Workflow timeline on Workspace or Campaign default  
- Evidence graphs and JSON viewers  
- Badge spam (more than one status pill per row)  
- "AI is thinking…" without specific work described  
- Duplicate attention items across Home, Workspace, Inbox with different copy  

### ALWAYS appear

- Emma presence on Workspace  
- Plain-language status (what + why)  
- Real data or honest empty state with one next step  
- One primary action per screen state  
- Peer accent (Marketing blue) used with restraint  
- Typography hierarchy — title before container  
- Review bar during any review flow (Product Bible law)  
- Consistent section header system across tabs  
- Spacing from 8/12/16/24/32/40 rhythm  
- Mobile-readable content — no clipped approval titles  

### Visual parity with Home

Marketing Workspace must feel like **the same operating system** as Home Command Center:

- Same executive card quality  
- Same briefing tone  
- Same KPI + chart ratio  
- Same approval module language  
- Same calm white space  

Different scope — same craft.

## 18. Emma Principles

Ten principles for every future Marketing Workspace decision:

**1. Outcome before process.**  
If a screen shows how Emma works before what Emma achieved, redesign the hierarchy.

**2. One glance, one decision.**  
Each arrival should enable at most one meaningful action — or confident silence.

**3. Speak like a colleague.**  
First person, specific, calm. No software dialect.

**4. Real or absent.**  
Metrics, charts, and claims require evidence — or honest gaps.

**5. Interrupt rarely, clearly.**  
Approvals are expensive attention events. Use them at real gates only.

**6. Briefing is the signature.**  
Emma's voice in the briefing band is the trust moment — protect its quality above features.

**7. Work is momentum, not inventory.**  
Campaign lists show movement and ownership — never database aesthetics.

**8. Content is visual.**  
Preview-first, publishing-first. No file metaphors.

**9. Process is opt-in.**  
Workflow exists for transparency — never as default hero content.

**10. Same office, different desk.**  
Every future peer reuses this architecture. Marketing proves the model; Sales, Support, and others inherit the skeleton.

## 19. Future Scalability

### The reusable skeleton

Every peer workspace ships the **same six destinations**:

| Tab | Marketing (Emma) | Sales | Support | Planner | Finance | CEO |
|-----|------------------|-------|---------|---------|---------|-----|
| **Workspace** | Marketing command center | Pipeline command center | Queue command center | Schedule command center | Close command center | Org command center |
| **Work** | Campaigns | Deals / Sequences | Cases | Plans / Milestones | Report runs | Initiatives |
| **Content** | Posts, emails, ads | Proposals, sequences | Responses, macros | Documents | Statements | Briefings |
| **Performance** | ROAS, leads, reach | Revenue, win rate | CSAT, resolution time | On-time % | Savings, accuracy | Strategic KPIs |
| **Workflow** | Marketing pipeline | Sales pipeline | Triage pipeline | Planning pipeline | Close pipeline | Synthesis pipeline |
| **Settings** | Working agreement | Working agreement | Working agreement | Working agreement | Working agreement | Working agreement |

**No redesign per peer.** Domain plugins supply: entity nouns, KPI templates, briefing accomplishments, pipeline stage labels, content preview types.

### Domain plugin contract (conceptual)

Each peer registers:

- `presenceSentence()` — current work narrative  
- `briefingAccomplishments()` — bullet lines  
- `heroKpi()` — primary business metric  
- `workGroups()` — business state groupings  
- `contentPreview()` — asset card renderer metadata  
- `performanceMetrics()` — connected metric definitions  
- `pipelineStages()` — customer-language workflow map  
- `settingsSections()` — domain-specific config blocks  

### Shared surfaces (org-level, not duplicated)

| Surface | Owner |
|---------|-------|
| Attention queue | Inbox (org) |
| Morning arrival | Home |
| Review experience | Shared Review surface + review bar |
| Integration OAuth | Settings → Connections (peer-scoped credentials) |
| Company truth | Company (org) — Emma reads, does not edit here |

### Migration from legacy Team workspace

Sprint 29B six-section model (Working on / Waiting / Done / Work / Results / Settings) **maps into** this bible:

| Legacy section | New home |
|----------------|----------|
| Working on | Workspace Band A + B |
| Waiting for me | Workspace Band D + Inbox |
| Done | Workspace Band G + Performance |
| Work | Work tab |
| Results | Performance tab |
| Settings | Settings tab |
| (missing) Content | Content tab |
| (missing) Workflow | Workflow tab |

No customer-facing regression: every legacy question remains answerable — faster, calmer, fewer tabs for delegation users.

### CEO and Analytics peers

**CEO Workspace** is org-wide Workspace at peer scope — reads aggregated signals, does not duplicate Home entirely. Briefings synthesize cross-peer.

**Analytics peer** (future) is **read-heavy Performance** with cross-domain charts — still uses six tabs; Work = reports/dashboards delivered; Content = exported artifacts.

### What does not scale (intentionally)

- Custom nav per peer  
- Custom band order per peer  
- Custom approval patterns per peer  
- Peer-specific shell chrome  

Variation lives in **words and data**, not **architecture**.
